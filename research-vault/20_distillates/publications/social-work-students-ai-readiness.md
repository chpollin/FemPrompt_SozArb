# Distillate: Are future social workers ready for AI? Fears, barriers, and learning needs in higher education

The mixed-methods study examines social-work students’ attitudes toward AI through a questionnaire, exploratory factor analysis and qualitative thematic analysis. Attitudes combine perceived utility, professional threat, ethical concern and emotional ambivalence.

## Research question and method

The exploratory mixed-methods study asks how social-work students perceive AI, which attitudinal dimensions structure their responses and what learning needs follow. It combines a 17-item attitude questionnaire, exploratory factor analysis and qualitative thematic analysis.

## Main findings

Students show neither simple acceptance nor rejection. Perceived utility coexists with fears of replacement and dehumanisation, while empathy, active listening and professional judgement are treated as non-delegable. Some non-use reflects distrust or lack of knowledge.

## Assessment relevance

`AI_Literacies`, `KI_Sonstige` and `Soziale_Arbeit` are central because the study examines student readiness and curriculum needs. `Bias_Ungleichheit` and `Fairness` are relevant to the recommended critical curriculum, but they are not measured outcomes.

## Limitations

The sample size and disciplinary composition limit generalisation. The preliminary attitude scale needs refinement, and the cross-sectional design cannot show how views change through education or professional experience.
