# Intersectional framing of AI inequalities

Ulnicane (2024), *Intersectionality in Artificial Intelligence: Framing Concerns and Recommendations for Action*, DOI 10.17645/si.7543, analyses how four reports published between 2019 and 2021 frame AI inequalities and proposed responses. The reports themselves were not separately read in this session. Statements about them below are attributed to this article.

## Core statements

- Ulnicane (2024, section 2.1) adopts intersectionality to examine multiple identity categories in relation to power, oppression and social hierarchy, explicitly discussing the challenge to binary gender and socially constructed identities. ^s1
  > “Examining the convergence of various social identity categories is crucial for understanding processes of oppression, dominance, and power.” (reading-layer line 41).
- Ulnicane (2024, section 3.3) reports that gender in the analysed reports is generally treated in relation to power structures and other social categories, while the available data can remain binary and insufficient for intersectional analysis. ^s2
  > “Gender is typically understood as non‐binary and like other social categories, it is analyzed in the context of existing power structures.” (reading-layer line 115).
- Ulnicane (2024, sections 2.2 and 4) uses a framing approach and limits the evidentiary reach of the examined reports because they originate in elite Global North institutions and predominantly involve senior professionals in a few countries. ^s3
  > “this study uses a framing approach” (reading-layer line 61).
  > “their data and methods mainly focus on senior professionals in a few most developed countries” (reading-layer line 151).
- Ulnicane (2024, sections 3.1 and 4) reconstructs the reports' diagnosis that workforce discrimination and biased AI systems are interconnected systemic problems. This is an analysis of report framings, not a causal test of an LLM intervention. ^s4
  > “issues of discrimination in the workforce and in system building are deeply intertwined” (quotation from a report reproduced by Ulnicane, reading-layer line 67).
