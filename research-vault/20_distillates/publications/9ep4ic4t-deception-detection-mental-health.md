# Distillate: Ethics and fairness of AI deception detection in mental health

## Core statements

Six virtual focus groups with 18 people who were both clinicians and current therapy clients discussed a hypothetical AI deception-detection scenario. Themes include therapeutic disruption, autonomy, power, confidentiality, false positives, cultural sensitivity and equitable deployment.

- Participants raised concerns that data inferred from unconscious behavioural cues could create privacy risks when storage, access and protection remain unclear. ^s1
  > "Confidentiality emerged as a central concern across all focus groups." (bound Version, RQ2 discussion)
- Participants warned that culturally specific, neurodivergent or emotionally regulated behaviour could be misread as deception, and that demographically skewed training data could produce unequal false positives. ^s2
  > "AI  systems  may  struggle  to  accurately  interpret nonverbal and paralinguistic cues of clients from underrepresented backgrounds, leading to higher rates of false positives or interpretive errors for certain groups." (bound Version, RQ3 discussion)
- Fair deployment requires attention to who is subjected to the tool, which authority selects them and whether discretionary use creates further stigma or power imbalance. ^s3
  > "equitable use policies" (bound Version, Abstract Results and RQ3 implications)
- The analysis combined deductive coding from the research questions and an earlier codebook with iterative refinement across the remaining transcripts. ^s4
  > "This revised structure was then applied to  the  remaining  transcripts  through  an  iterative  process  of independent  coding,  with  the  codebook  refined  as  needed." (bound Version, Qualitative Analysis)
## Method and scope

The study asks which ethical safeguards should govern integration, how confidentiality and trust can be preserved, and which design and evaluation strategies can mitigate bias. It conducted six 90-minute virtual focus groups with 18 participants who were both mental health clinicians and current therapy clients. A semistructured protocol centred on a hypothetical multimodal deception-detection system. Two independent raters began with deductive coding of three transcripts, reconciled and refined the codebook, iteratively coded the remaining transcripts and completed a final consistency pass. The reported themes concern integration, data confidentiality and AI integrity.
