# Distillate: Generative KI in der Beratung: Empirische Befunde mit Blick auf Nutzungsbedingungen von Chatbots

The article uses empirical findings about digital social and debt counselling to discuss conditions that could matter for future chatbot integration. The empirical data concern digital access, counselling-format preferences and professional experience with hybrid counselling. Its chatbot claims are transfer and outlook arguments.

## Core statements

- The article transfers findings from digital social and debt counselling to the prospective design of AI-supported counselling. ^s1
  > "individual concepts are needed for the design of counselling sessions that are geared towards resources and needs of clients" (10.21240/dimesoz/2026/1/57)

- The empirical material supports counselling concepts tailored to clients’ resources and needs while professionals renegotiate their role in digitally mediated counselling. ^s2
  > "professionals are facing a redefinition of their roles in terms of professionalisation" (10.21240/dimesoz/2026/1/57)

- The study collected no chatbot-specific data, so its chatbot discussion remains prospective. ^s3
  > "Daten zu Chatbots im Konkreten wurden in der vorliegenden Studie nicht erhoben, da die Nutzung von KI-Anwendungen zum Erhebungszeitpunkt noch nicht flächendeckend vorhanden war." (10.21240/dimesoz/2026/1/57, limitations and outlook)

## Research question and method

The article asks which conditions of counselling practice should be considered when generative-AI chatbots are introduced. Its empirical basis is broader digital counselling. A pencil-and-paper survey conducted in late 2022 covered 165 users of social and debt counselling and asked about device ownership, media use, digital skills and preferences for counselling formats. Four group discussions with social- and debt-counselling teams, held before and after measures to establish hybrid counselling structures, were analysed through qualitative content analysis. The study did not collect chatbot-specific data.

## Main findings

The survey shows variation in digital access, media-related skills and preferences for counselling formats. The group discussions show different professional attitudes and experiences with digital counselling and a need for training, reflection and organisational support. The authors use these findings to argue that chatbot-supported counselling would require individual concepts, bridge structures for clients at risk of digital exclusion and continued professional responsibility. These are transfer and outlook arguments rather than observed effects of chatbot use.

## Assessment relevance

`Generative_KI` is central as the prospective application discussed by the article, and `Soziale_Arbeit` is central through social and debt counselling. `AI_Literacies` is relevant because the article explicitly discusses media-related skills among clients as well as training and reflective use among professionals. The source does not establish a prompting intervention or a comparative fairness evaluation.

## Limitations

The evidence comes from particular social- and debt-counselling contexts and concerns digital and hybrid counselling. No chatbot-specific data were collected because AI applications were not yet widely used at the time of data collection. The transfer arguments therefore do not demonstrate chatbot uptake, effectiveness or safety. The client survey also excludes people who used only the services’ digital formats.
