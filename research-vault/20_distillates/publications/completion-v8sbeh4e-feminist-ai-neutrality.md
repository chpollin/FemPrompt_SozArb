# Distillate: Algorithm beyond Neutrality: Feminist Approaches to Redefining Artificial Intelligence

The conference chapter argues that artificial-intelligence systems reflect social power and historical bias rather than neutral computation. It combines a short feminist literature discussion with a small local survey about attitudes toward gender bias and feminist principles in AI development.

## Core statements

- The chapter frames algorithmic neutrality as an illusion because data and design are socially constructed. ^s1
  > "Neutrality as illusion: Multiple feminist researchers state that neutrality is unachievable as the date, design in the AI system is socially constructed." (10.2991/978-94-6239-618-0_20, section 3.5)
- Feminist approaches are presented as tools for analysing power, intersectionality, exclusion and responsibility in AI development. ^s2
- The empirical component is an online Likert-scale survey of a small, locally bounded sample recruited through social media. ^s3
  > "The study adopts quantitative research design using a survey method to explore public perspective on gender bias artificial intelligence and role of feminist perspectives in development of AI." (10.2991/978-94-6239-618-0_20, section 4.1)
- The chapter recommends participation by women and marginalised groups in AI teams and education that combines technical and critical theory. ^s4
  > "The recruit of women and marginalized groups in the AI should be promoted." (10.2991/978-94-6239-618-0_20, section 4.10)

## Research question and method

The chapter asks which ideas feminist researchers offer for challenging claims of AI neutrality and how surveyed participants view the inclusion of feminist ideas in AI systems. It reviews selected writing on cyberfeminism, data feminism, intersectionality and inclusive design. The empirical component is a purpose-built online questionnaire with a five-point Likert scale. Eleven adult participants from Dehradun were recruited through social-media distribution. The paper describes the sample as randomised, although the reported recruitment route does not establish probability sampling.

## Main findings

The literature discussion presents bias as a product of training data, design choices and unequal participation. It argues that intersectional analysis, co-design, accountability and more diverse development teams can expose and reduce exclusions that a neutrality claim conceals.

The survey responses are interpreted as support for recognising AI bias, regulating systems for gender equality and including feminist principles in development. Given the recruitment method and sample, these results describe the participating group and do not establish wider public opinion.

## Assessment relevance

`KI_Sonstige`, `Gender`, `Feministisch`, `Bias_Ungleichheit`, `Diversitaet` and `Fairness` are central because the chapter applies feminist and intersectional concepts to gendered algorithmic power, inclusion and equitable AI design. `AI_Literacies` is relevant through the recommendation that development teams learn feminist and racial theory alongside machine learning. The chapter does not concern social work, generative AI or prompting.

## Limitations

The literature review reports no search or selection protocol. The survey has eleven participants from one city, most of them students, recruited through social media. The instrument measures stated attitudes and includes only a shallow measure of familiarity with feminist critique. The sampling description is inconsistent with the recruitment procedure, and the paper reports no inferential test of its stated hypotheses. Conversion QC remained incomplete during preparation. Figures showing survey results and visual layout were not independently inspected, so this distillate makes no figure-dependent quantitative claims.
