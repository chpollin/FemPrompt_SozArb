# Distillate: ReGiFT as reasoning-guided fine-tuning

In arXiv:2504.05632v3, dated 5 June 2025, Kabra, Jha and Reddy investigate whether general reasoning supervision affects stereotypical responses. This distillate describes the method; it claims neither general effectiveness nor completed domain-expert verification.

## Core statements

- ReGiFT uses reasoning traces generated by a reasoning model as training supervision for base models; the transfer occurs through fine-tuning. ^s1
  > "we use these traces to fine-tune the base models" (arxiv:2504.05632v3, sec. 3)

- The authors explicitly distinguish this learned reasoning from Chain-of-Thought prompting, which elicits reasoning only at inference time. ^s2
  > "we treat reasoning as a learned capability" (arxiv:2504.05632v3, sec. 3.2)

- SQuAD-v2 provides training supervision, whereas the examined BBQ subsets are used exclusively for evaluation. ^s3
  > "no fine-tuning is performed on BBQ" (arxiv:2504.05632v3, sec. 4.1)

## Method and study scope

DeepSeek Distill Qwen 2.5 32B generates formatted reasoning traces and answers on SQuAD-v2. For the main procedure, the authors retain examples with correct final answers and train base models on these data (sections 3.1–3.2, Algorithm 1). They evaluate both ambiguous and disambiguated contexts in the age, religion and nationality subsets of BBQ (section 4). ReGiFT is therefore a training intervention; the evaluated CoT condition is a separate prompting baseline (section 5.2).

## Limitations

- Selection by correct final answer serves as a proxy for trace quality. It does not establish the validity of the reasoning trace itself; section 3.1 and footnote 2 acknowledge this assumption.
- The findings concern the described models, tasks and three BBQ subsets. They establish neither general bias elimination nor demonstrated benefits for social work (section 4, Ethics Statement, Limitations).
- The examined source is preprint v3. The PDF describes itself as under review; this distillate does not replace an assessment of study quality.
- The original figures were also read. Figure 4 shows error bars without defining their dispersion measure; no dispersion values or significance tests are inferred from them here.
