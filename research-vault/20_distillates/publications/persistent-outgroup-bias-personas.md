# Distillate: I Am Not Them: Persistent Outgroup Bias in Large Language Models Arising from Social Identity Persona Setting

The study applies social identity theory to prompted personas and reports ingroup favouritism and outgroup bias across political and gender domains. It also tests identity reassignment as a prompt-level mitigation.

## Core statements

- The experiment measures both ingroup favouritism and negative treatment of outgroups under persona prompts. ^s1
  > "self-categorization of social identity leads to both ingroup favoritism and outgroup bias" (10.63317/2hn5gs6yh5m2)

- The authors explicitly delimit transfer beyond the evaluated identities, domains or runs. ^s2
  > "our work is limited to the political and gender domains, the English language, and the US context" (10.63317/2hn5gs6yh5m2)

## Research question and method

The study asks whether prompted social identities induce ingroup favouritism and outgroup bias in language models. Guided by social identity theory, it evaluates political and gender personas with survey-style Likert measures and tests reassignment to a disfavoured-group identity as mitigation.

## Main findings

The reported outputs show both ingroup favouritism and outgroup derogation, with outgroup bias at least as strong in the evaluated political and gender settings. Reassigning the model to the initially disfavoured group changes alignment and can reduce the measured pre-existing bias.

## Assessment relevance

`Generative_KI`, `Prompting`, `Bias_Ungleichheit`, `Gender` and `Fairness` are central. `Diversitaet` is relevant through intergroup treatment, but the empirical domains remain narrow. Social work is absent.

## Limitations

The evidence is restricted to political and gender domains, English, a United States context, selected models and group-level averages. Likert-anchored responses do not cover open-ended downstream behaviour.
