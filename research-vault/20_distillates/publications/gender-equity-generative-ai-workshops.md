# Distillate: Beyond “painting in pink”: A critical case study of all-girls generative AI workshops in a European makerspace and implications for gender equity in computing

The qualitative case study examines all-girls generative-AI workshops in a European makerspace. It finds positive views of the format alongside barriers in topic choice, peer collaboration, tool accessibility and prompt-writing language.

## Core statements

- The qualitative case study examines all-girls generative-AI workshops in a European makerspace. ^s1
  > "Equity-oriented uses of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) remain largely underexplored." (10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100602)

- The authors argue for interventions that centre girls’ voices, agency and assets across symbolic, structural, social and individual levels. ^s2
  > "We argue that gender-equity interventions in computing need to move beyond the superficial" (10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100602)

## Research question and method

The qualitative case study asks how girls, parents and practitioners perceive an all-girls format and how practitioners understand gender-equity work in workshops using image-generative tools. Evidence includes interviews, reflective sessions, observations and generated images, interpreted through a multilevel gender framework and feminist pedagogy.

## Main findings

Participants generally valued the all-girls format, and practitioners used representation, relationship building and responsive support. Inaccessible tools, prompt-writing language, limited peer collaboration and an unsuitable topic nevertheless constrained girls’ voice and agency.

## Limitations

The small sample and one makerspace limit transfer. The study reports situated perceptions and practices rather than comparative learning effects, and it did not include non-binary or trans participants.
