# Distillate: ChatGPT for social work science: Ethical challenges and opportunities

The invited paper considers how ChatGPT could assist social-work research while introducing bias, privacy, attribution and academic-integrity problems. It calls for explicit disclosure, human validation and a profession-wide ethical framework grounded in social justice.

## Core statements

- ChatGPT may assist with research questions, qualitative themes, transcript editing and presentations, but it should remain subordinate to contextual human judgement. ^s1
  > "As such, ChatGPT is best suited as an assistive technology tool for social work scientists." (10.1086/726042, Academic Misconduct)
- Human validation is required before AI-edited qualitative material enters professional or scientific dissemination. ^s2
  > "However, such edits require human validation prior to professional or scienti /uniFB01 c dissemination." (10.1086/726042, Qualitative Data Editor)
- Transparent use requires disclosure of the system, prompts, location of use, conflicts and limitations. ^s3
  > "Academic writing must clearly document the use of ChatGPT/LLMs, highlight which LLM was used, describe and disclose all potential con /uniFB02 icts of interest," (10.1086/726042, Transparency)
- The authors connect responsible use with antiracist research practice and attention to literature from underrepresented communities. ^s4
  > "Social work scientists must be aware of social justice and antiracist principles when using LLMs." (10.1086/726042, Inclusion and Social Justice)

## Research question and method

The paper asks which opportunities ChatGPT offers social-work science, which ethical problems accompany its use and which preliminary safeguards the profession should adopt. It is an invited conceptual commentary. The authors discuss prospective research uses, analyse limitations and formulate five recommendations covering transparency, fact-checking, authorship, attribution, and inclusion and social justice. It contains examples and normative argument rather than a new empirical study or systematic evidence synthesis.

## Main findings

The paper identifies prospective uses in question generation, simulations, qualitative theme development, transcript editing, presentations and feedback on biased language. It argues that these functions require human validation because ChatGPT lacks local context and nuance, can reproduce oppressive institutional assumptions and may return unsourced or inaccurate material.

The recommendations require disclosure of model use and prompts, verification and citation of sources, retention of human authorship, attribution of underlying ideas, and active efforts to include scholarship and perspectives from marginalised communities. The paper also calls on social-work organisations to develop a shared ethical framework for large language models.

## Limitations

The paper offers prospective examples and preliminary recommendations from 2023. It does not evaluate the accuracy, research quality or social-justice effects of any ChatGPT-supported workflow. Claims about access benefits and inclusive prompting are proposals rather than demonstrated outcomes. The Docling conversion contains extraction artefacts such as `/uniFB01` and compressed spacing.
