# Social-work education sources frame AI literacy as critical understanding beyond technical competence

## Statement

Three social-work education sources describe the digital or AI literacy that social workers need as a combination of technical understanding with critical understanding of the social, ethical and political effects of the technology.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/qrk7c3jk-critical-digital-literacy-social-work-ai#^s1]] frames digital literacy as a capacity to understand, question and navigate technology rather than technical competence alone.
- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-qi5aye4v-algorithmic-decision-making-pedagogy#^s1]] describes a dual educational task of technical competence and critique of the social framing and effects of algorithmic systems.
- [[20_distillates/publications/social-work-students-ai-readiness#^s2]] calls for critical AI literacy in curricula that joins technical understanding with ethics, relational competence and professional judgement.

## Scope boundary

The three sources are conceptual proposals or derive learning needs from a student attitude survey. None evaluates a curriculum, a learning outcome or professional practice. The Assertion reports what the sources name as needed, not that the named literacy has been shown to improve practice. Contradicting sources were not systematically searched; `contested-with: []` does not assert consensus.
