# Cheng et al. report that workers used context to compensate for model limitations

## Statement

Interviews and contextual inquiry by Cheng et al. (2022) in one county child-welfare intake system suggest that workers used contextual information, compensated for perceived limitations of a screening model and sometimes collaborated across roles.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/7fefmcbz-child-welfare-worker-algorithm-disparities#^s3]] supports the qualitative finding on worker practice.
- [[20_distillates/publications/7fefmcbz-child-welfare-worker-algorithm-disparities#^s4]] qualifies the quantitative comparison, which does not establish that the tool caused the observed change.

## Scope boundary

The finding concerns one county, one workflow and one predictive tool, not generative AI. The qualitative fieldwork took place after policy and model changes and does not cover the period of the quantitative comparison. The Assertion records described worker practice and makes no claim that worker discretion generally reduces disparities.
