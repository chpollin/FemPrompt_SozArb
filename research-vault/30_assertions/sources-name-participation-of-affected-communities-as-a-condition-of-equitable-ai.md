# Sources name participation of affected communities as a condition of equitable AI

## Statement

Sources from social work, gender equity, digital equity and intersectional fairness name the participation of affected communities or stakeholders as a condition for equitable AI education, design or governance.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/zh6t88d5-visual-disinformation-digital-equity#^s4]]. Putri (2026) requires media-literacy interventions to be co-designed with target communities and to use local knowledge.
- [[20_distillates/publications/quv5dqh3-gender-equity-algorithms#^s3]]. Smith and Rustagi (2021) include participation by affected communities in their proposed response to gender bias.
- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-shjqqti6-intersectional-fairness-machine-learning#^s3]]. Gohar and Cheng (2023) call for broader stakeholder involvement in defining intersectional fairness notions.
- [[20_distillates/publications/epic-model-ai-social-work#^s1]]. Baker et al. (2025) include community engagement and empowerment in the EPIC model.

## Scope boundary

The sources address different levels, namely literacy provision for communities, system design and fairness definitions, and a practice model. Participation is a named requirement in each, and none reports an evaluation of a participatory arrangement. The participation concerns affected communities, which differs from the competences of individual professionals.
