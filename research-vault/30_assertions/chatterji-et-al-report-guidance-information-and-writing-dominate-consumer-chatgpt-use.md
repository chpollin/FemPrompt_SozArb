# Chatterji et al. report that guidance, information seeking and writing dominate consumer ChatGPT use

## Statement

Chatterji et al. (2025) report that practical guidance, seeking information and writing account for most classified consumer ChatGPT conversations, based on automated LLM classification of messages whose content the research team did not inspect.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/bhxdu7vm-how-people-use-chatgpt#^s2]] supports the three most common conversation topics and their joint share of classified conversations.
- [[20_distillates/publications/bhxdu7vm-how-people-use-chatgpt#^s4]] supports that the research team did not look at message content, so the topic shares rest on automated classification.

## Scope boundary

The study describes general consumer use of one system and does not examine social work, professional tasks of social workers or benefits of use. According to the distillate's limitations section, which carries no statement anchor, it omits business, enterprise and education plans, inherits classifier error, and is an NBER working paper without peer review. Its occupational analysis of work-related use has no statement anchor in the distillate and is not part of this Assertion. For SQ1 the Assertion serves only as a general reference profile of LLM tasks against which social-work-specific evidence can later be compared.
