# Patton et al. and Victor et al. describe prospective LLM uses in social-work research

## Statement

Two conceptual contributions on social-work research, Patton et al. (2023) and Victor et al. (2023), describe the use of large language models for research tasks as prospective assistive use, naming the generation of research questions, the development of qualitative themes, transcript editing and presentations as well as information extraction, classification and clustering in analysis.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-zitlbm8a-chatgpt-social-work-science#^s1]] supports the four research tasks named by Patton et al. and their framing of ChatGPT as an assistive tool subordinate to contextual human judgement.
- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-amyzfaph-generative-ai-social-work-research#^s3]] supports information extraction, classification and clustering as analysis tasks for which Victor et al. expect researchers to employ LLMs. The statement itself is a reporting recommendation that names these tasks.

## Scope boundary

The setting is social-work research, not social-work practice. Both papers are invited conceptual or normative contributions from 2023. According to the method and limitation sections of both distillates, which carry no statement anchors, neither paper evaluates the accuracy, research quality or effects of an LLM-supported workflow, and the benefits they name are proposals rather than demonstrated outcomes. The wider list of uses in the main findings of the Victor et al. distillate (literature searching, writing, qualitative coding, statistical work, programming and dissemination) has no statement anchor and is therefore not part of this Assertion. Contradicting sources were not systematically searched, so `contested-with: []` does not assert consensus.
