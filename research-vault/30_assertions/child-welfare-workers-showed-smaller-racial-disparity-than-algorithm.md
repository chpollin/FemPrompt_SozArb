# Worker-assisted child-welfare screening showed a smaller racial disparity than an algorithm-only policy

## Statement

In one county's child-welfare referral screening, a mixed-methods study reports that worker decisions assisted by the Allegheny Family Screening Tool produced a smaller Black-white disparity in screen-in rates than a hypothetical algorithm-only policy based on the same tool, without establishing that the tool caused the change.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/7fefmcbz-child-welfare-worker-algorithm-disparities#^s1]] supports the setting and the comparison of worker-assisted decisions with a hypothetical algorithm-only policy.
- [[20_distillates/publications/7fefmcbz-child-welfare-worker-algorithm-disparities#^s2]] supports the direction of the disparity comparison, which the source reports as 20 percent for the algorithm-only policy and 9 percent for worker-assisted decisions in the studied post-deployment period.
- [[20_distillates/publications/7fefmcbz-child-welfare-worker-algorithm-disparities#^s3]] supports the qualitative component, in which workers used contextual information and compensated for perceived model limitations.
- [[20_distillates/publications/7fefmcbz-child-welfare-worker-algorithm-disparities#^s4]] supports the causal limit.

## Scope boundary

Affected group: Black and white children referred to one county child-welfare intake. System: a predictive risk model, not a generative language model. Measure: screen-in-rate disparity of an observed and a counterfactual decision rule. Study type: mixed-methods study with a counterfactual quantitative comparison, interviews and contextual inquiry. The distillate's limitations section adds that the algorithm-only rule depends on a chosen threshold, that the source does not separate warranted from unwarranted disparity and that the findings concern one workflow and tool version. The Assertion makes no claim that worker discretion generally reduces algorithmic disparity. `contested-with: []` records that no conflicting statement was found among the active distillates and does not assert consensus.
