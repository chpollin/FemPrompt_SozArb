# Bias evaluations name their binary gender operationalisation as a limitation

## Statement

Three bias evaluations of language or vision-and-language models state that their binary or predefined treatment of gender and identity does not represent non-binary, diverse or intersectional identities.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-z9dntbff-gender-bias-vision-language-models#^s4]] supports that the authors call their binary male and female terms a serious limitation and ask for more inclusive probes and data.
- [[20_distillates/publications/persona-motivated-reasoning#^s2]] supports, through its quotation, that binary gender categories do not represent the full range of diverse identities.
- [[20_distillates/publications/multi-persona-thinking-bias-mitigation#^s2]] supports, through its quotation, that identities are often complex, non-binary and intersectional, which the distillate records as a limitation of predefined binary personas.

## Scope boundary

Affected groups: non-binary, trans and gender-nonconforming people and people with intersecting identities, who remain outside the measured categories. Systems: a vision-and-language model and several LLMs under persona prompts. Study type: probing study and two experiments. For the second and third source the anchored statement is a general transfer limit and the specific content sits in its quotation. The Assertion reports the authors' own limitation statements. It does not claim that the measured bias would be larger or smaller under inclusive operationalisations. `contested-with: []` records that no conflicting statement was found among the active distillates and does not assert consensus.
