# Cheng et al. and Field et al. examine predictive and NLP tools in child welfare

## Statement

Cheng et al. (2022) and Field et al. (2023) examine algorithmic tools in county child-welfare practice, namely a predictive screening score that assists intake decisions and natural-language processing applied to administrative contact notes.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/7fefmcbz-child-welfare-worker-algorithm-disparities#^s1]] supports the intake setting and the comparison of a score-based policy with worker decisions assisted by the Allegheny Family Screening Tool.
- [[20_distillates/publications/r7v99era-racial-bias-nlp-child-protective-services#^s1]] supports the application of natural-language processing to administrative contact notes of one county child-protection system.

## Scope boundary

Neither tool is a large language model. The assessment-relevance sections of both distillates, which carry no statement anchors, state that the studies do not examine generative AI, and the Field et al. distillate records that the paper's discussion of generative models is prospective. For SQ1 the Assertion therefore marks the task settings of the empirical child-welfare evidence in the active distillates as adjacent algorithmic decision support and information extraction rather than LLM use. Each study concerns one county and its own tool version.
