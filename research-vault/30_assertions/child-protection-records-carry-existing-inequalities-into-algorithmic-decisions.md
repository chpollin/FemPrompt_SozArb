# Child-protection records carry existing inequalities into algorithmic decisions

## Statement

Two child-protection sources argue that administrative records reflect reporting, surveillance and prior institutional decisions rather than the incidence of harm to children, so that predictive or text-processing systems built on these records can reproduce existing system disparities, which the conceptual analysis names as racial and class inequalities.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-a2p8mxmy-algorithmic-justice-child-protection#^s1]] supports that administrative data do not represent population incidence and that reporting, surveillance, service access and institutional decisions shape the data and the training labels.
- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-a2p8mxmy-algorithmic-justice-child-protection#^s2]] supports the reproduction of racial and class inequalities through proxy outcomes, feedback loops and correlations.
- [[20_distillates/publications/r7v99era-racial-bias-nlp-child-protective-services#^s3]] supports that case notes may encode perception, surveillance and prior decisions and that extraction can deepen institutional information asymmetries.

## Scope boundary

Affected groups: families in Anglophone child-protection systems, with racial and class inequalities named in the conceptual analysis and racial disparity examined in the empirical study of one county. Systems: predictive risk models and natural-language processing of case notes, not generative language models. Study types: a critical conceptual analysis and the discussion section of an empirical evaluation. The Assertion states a mechanism argued by the sources. It does not quantify the size of the reproduced inequality, which the sources tie to jurisdiction, dataset, workflow and tool design. `contested-with: []` records that no conflicting statement was found among the active distillates and does not assert consensus.
