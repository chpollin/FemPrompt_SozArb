# Sources locate responsible AI use in governance and quality-control structures

## Statement

Several sources locate responsible AI use partly in organisational and professional structures, naming ethics and policy frameworks, documentation, audits, gender-sensitive governance and editorial quality control.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/epic-model-ai-social-work#^s1]]. Baker et al. (2025) propose the EPIC model with the components ethics and justice, policy development and advocacy, intersectoral collaboration, and community engagement and empowerment.
- [[20_distillates/publications/quv5dqh3-gender-equity-algorithms#^s3]]. Smith and Rustagi (2021) combine feminist data practices, participation, gender expertise, documentation, audits and gender-sensitive governance.
- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-amyzfaph-generative-ai-social-work-research#^s4]]. Victor et al. (2023) assign journal editors quality-control procedures and ethical standards for AI use in social-work research.

## Scope boundary

All three sources are conceptual or practice-oriented proposals. None evaluates the proposed structures. Smith and Rustagi (2021) address algorithm design and governance across sectors, not social work. The Assertion records where the sources place responsibility, not that these structures are effective.
