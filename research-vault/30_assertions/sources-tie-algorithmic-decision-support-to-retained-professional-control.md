# Sources tie algorithmic decision support to retained professional control

## Statement

Two child-welfare sources treat retained professional or team control over the decision as a condition for implementing algorithmic decision support.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-a2p8mxmy-algorithmic-justice-child-protection#^s4]]. Keddell (2019) argues that implementation and management of tools must help workers keep ultimate control over decisions for which they remain accountable.
- [[20_distillates/publications/exrf5629-child-welfare-placement-prediction#^s4]]. Trudeau et al. (2023) present their placement predictions as one input to team deliberation, with responsibility retained by the team.

## Scope boundary

Both sources concern predictive machine learning in child welfare, not generative AI. Keddell (2019) is a critical conceptual analysis, while Trudeau et al. (2023) develop the predictive models and state the boundary as part of their design. The sources share the condition but differ in their assessment of predictive tools. Neither evaluates whether retained control prevents harm.
