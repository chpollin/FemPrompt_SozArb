# Sources tie fair deployment to the rights and power position of those subjected to AI

## Statement

Two sources from mental-health and child-protection contexts tie the fairness of AI deployment to the position of the people subjected to it, including who selects them, their rights to participate and challenge, and the power imbalance the use creates.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/9ep4ic4t-deception-detection-mental-health#^s3]]. King et al. (2026) report from focus groups that fair deployment requires attention to who is subjected to the tool, which authority selects them and whether discretionary use creates stigma or power imbalance.
- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-a2p8mxmy-algorithmic-justice-child-protection#^s3]]. Keddell (2019) argues that statistical fairness criteria do not settle due process, participation, consent and the right to challenge an inference.

## Scope boundary

King et al. (2026) report stakeholder views on a hypothetical deception-detection technology, and Keddell (2019) is a conceptual analysis of predictive tools. Neither concerns generative AI directly. The cited statement from Keddell (2019) carries no attached quotation and needs confirmation against the source text in review.
