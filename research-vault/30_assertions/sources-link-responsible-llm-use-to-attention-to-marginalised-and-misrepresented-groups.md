# Sources link responsible LLM use to attention to marginalised and misrepresented groups

## Statement

Two social-work sources make attention to underrepresented, marginalised or misrepresented groups part of responsible engagement with generative AI and its outputs.

## Support

- [[20_distillates/publications/completion-zitlbm8a-chatgpt-social-work-science#^s4]]. Patton et al. (2023) connect responsible LLM use in social-work science with social-justice and antiracist principles and with attention to literature from underrepresented communities.
- [[20_distillates/publications/qrk7c3jk-critical-digital-literacy-social-work-ai#^s4]]. Wallengren-Lynch (2026) formulates teaching questions that direct attention to who or what is missing, marginalised or misrepresented in generated images.

## Scope boundary

The first source is a normative commentary on research use, and the second uses five one-time image generations as classroom material without a sampling or coding protocol. Neither evaluates whether such attention changes outputs or decisions. The Assertion reports a named orientation, not a demonstrated competence.
