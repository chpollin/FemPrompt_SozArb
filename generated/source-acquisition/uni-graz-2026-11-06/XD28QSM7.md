## Accuracy and Readability of Large Language Models ' Responses to Frequently Asked Questions on Social Assistance

Hacer Ta ş k ı ran Tepe Bilecik Ş eyh Edebali University

ABSTRACT Objective: Arti /uniFB01 cial-intelligence-powered large language models (LLMs) are reshaping information access and increasingly serve as digital sources for publicservice-related content, including social assistance. However, concerns remain re­ garding the accuracy and readability of the information LLMs generate, particularly for vulnerable populations. This study examines the accuracy and readability of re­ sponses produced by ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot to fre­ quently asked questions about social assistance programs in Türkiye. Method: Using 20 questions selected from the of /uniFB01 cial website of the Ministry of Family and Social Services, LLM-generated responses were collected in June 2024 and evaluated with a researcher-developed Likert-type scale for accuracy and the Ate ş man Readability Formula for readability. Results: The models showed limited performance in both do­ mains, with ChatGPT-3.5 producing the lowest averages in accuracy and readability. Gemini achieved the highest readability scores, whereas Microsoft Copilot performed best in terms of accuracy. ChatGPT-4o demonstrated moderate performance across both metrics. I found no signi /uniFB01 cant relationship between accuracy and readability. Conclusions: These results highlight the need to assess LLMs not only for their technical ac­ curacy but also for their ability to produce accessible and comprehensible content, particularly in the context of public information on social assistance.

## Arti /uniFB01 cial Intelligence in Social Work: A Review of the Literature

The integration of technology into social work practice has long been at the center of disciplinary discussions. AI and LLMs are increasingly visible in social work ed­ ucation, research, and practice. A review of the literature shows that studies tend to converge around three main thematic directions.

The /uniFB01 rst thematic direction consists of broad, mostly conceptual discussions concerning the potential bene /uniFB01 ts and risks of AI. This body of literature highlights the capacity of AI tools to strengthen decision-support processes, facilitate access, and enhance data-driven interventions while drawing attention to risks such as algorithmic bias, privacy, transparency, and professional boundaries (Ibrahim et al., 2023; Karago­ dina &amp; Semigina, 2025; Patton et al., 2023; Victor, Sokol, et al., 2023). A signi /uniFB01 cant milestone in this early period of discussion is Tambe and Rice ' s (2018) Arti /uniFB01 cial Intel­ ligence and Social Work guide, which addressed the structural implications of technology in social work practice and presented one of the foundational conceptual frame­ works for AI integration. The second thematic direction focuses on the use of AI and LLMs in social work education and academic research, examining their pedagog­ ical contributions, limitations, and effects on students ' digital competencies (Ahn et al., 2025; Al-Rantisi et al., 2025; Báez et al., 2025; Netto, 2023; Singer et al., 2023; Victor, Sokol, et al., 2023; Victor, Kuziak, et al., 2023). The third thematic di­ rection centers on early examples of AI use in practice settings and includes studies examining the role of AI in application assessment and service referral processes (Molala, 2025; Ta ş k ı ran Tepe &amp; Aslantürk, 2025).

International professional standards provide a guiding framework for ethics and risk management in the AI context. The Standards for Technology in Social Work Practice (National Association of Social Workers, Association of Social Work Boards, Council on Social Work Education, &amp; Clinical Social Work Association, 2017) outline key responsibilities for the ethical, safe, and inclusive use of digital technologies in professional practice, including privacy, informed consent, con /uniFB01 dentiality, and cul­ tural competence. The Global Ethical Principles of the International Federation of Social Workers (2018) similarly emphasize the protection of human dignity, social justice, and human rights across all practice environments, including digital con­ texts. Reamer ' s (2025) recent work on AI ethics demonstrates that the rapid expansion of AI in social work and behavioral health has introduced new ethical risks, such as challenges related to informed consent, privacy, client surveillance, transpar­ ency, algorithmic bias, and digital misconduct. Drawing on numerous case exam­ ples, Reamer ' s comprehensive analysis highlights the necessity of structuring AI-based tools in a manner that is careful, responsible, and aligned with professional standards -especially when working with vulnerable populations. Taken together, this framework illustrates that in the digital transformation of the social work profes­ sion, the focus extends not only to the use of technological tools but also to strength­ ening social justice through ethical practice, professional accountability, and principles of digital inclusion.

The existing literature indicates that social work ' s person -environment and structural justice perspectives are insuf /uniFB01 ciently represented in discussions on AI (Victor &amp; Herrenkohl, 2025). Despite the rapid expansion of AI, research on its poten­ tial applications in social work is nascent (Molala, 2025). Studies are concentrated in

narrow sub /uniFB01 elds such as child protection, youth services, and mental health (Gar­ kisch &amp; Goldkind, 2025), and most focus on predictive models (Wykman, 2023). Em­ pirical studies that systematically evaluate LLM performance are particularly scarce in domains such as social assistance, where legislation changes frequently and infor­ mation asymmetry is high.

The availability of effective, accessible, and comprehensible public information about social assistance is critical in the /uniFB01 ght against poverty and social exclusion. Access to accurate and clear information about such social assistance enables individuals in need to fully understand the process and make the most of their enti­ tlements. In recent years, LLMs have increasingly become one of the sources used to obtain information in many domains, including social assistance. Therefore, the ac­ curacy and readability of the information provided by LLMs concerning social assis­ tance programs are essential for ensuring that individuals can bene /uniFB01 t from these services.

## Accuracy and Readability Performance of Large Language Models: Existing Literature and Research Gaps

Readability refers to how easily a text can be understood and read (DuBay, 2004). El­ ements such as sentence structure, word choice, and the overall complexity of the language signi /uniFB01 cantly in /uniFB02 uence users ' ability to comprehend information (Ate ş man, 1997). Accuracy is the extent to which the provided information aligns with reality. In sensitive domains such as social assistance, incorrect or incomplete information may hinder individuals ' access to services, create obstacles during application processes, and exacerbate social inequalities. For this reason, assessing the accuracy and read­ ability of LLM outputs on social assistance topics is crucial in enhancing the reliabil­ ity of these technologies and maximizing their societal bene /uniFB01 t.

A substantial proportion of studies examining LLM accuracy and readability have concentrated on the /uniFB01 elds of medicine and health. These studies indicate that although models can achieve high accuracy in certain topics, performance varies de­ pending on the model type, question format, and contextual factors (Ayers et al., 2023; Buldur &amp; Sezer, 2024; Seth et al., 2023). Comparative research has shown that certain models provide higher accuracy in speci /uniFB01 c domains, such as dentistry and radiology (Giannakopoulos et al., 2023; Tepe &amp; Emekli, 2024a), whereas in other domains, such as communication sciences, no signi /uniFB01 cant differences are observed among models (Zhang et al., 2024). Findings related to readability are similarly inconsistent: Some studies report that particular models generate more comprehensible texts (Seth et al., 2023; Tepe &amp; Emekli, 2024a, 2024b), whereas others indicate that both institutional sources and widely used models exhibit low readability at a basic level (Srinivasan et al., 2024). Taken together, these /uniFB01 ndings suggest that although LLMs demonstrate certain strengths in the medical /uniFB01 eld, they do not exhibit consistent performance in terms of accuracy and readability, and that outcomes are highly context sensitive.

In the social work literature, studies examining the accuracy and readability of LLMs are quite limited (Ta ş k ı ran Tepe &amp; Arslantürk, 2025), and no original study speci /uniFB01 cally focusing on social assistance has been identi /uniFB01 ed -a signi /uniFB01 cant gap in the current literature. Understanding AI ' s potential to deliver information on existing social assistance programs and identifying how these systems can be best optimized for program applicants is a critical development in social aid and service delivery. This is because access to fundamental social rights -such as social as­ sistance -is a key component of individual empowerment. According to the em­ powerment approach, which holds a signi /uniFB01 cant place in the social work literature, access to information is not merely a technical requirement but a transformative tool that enables individuals to gain greater control over their lives, advocate for their rights, and participate in decision-making (Lee, 2001). Therefore, accurate and readable content provided by LLMs could enhance cognitive accessibility and informed decision-making capacity among individuals, thereby fostering social justice. In the future, institutions providing social assistance services may lever­ age AI technologies to streamline application processes and ensure the dissem­ ination of accurate and comprehensible information. However, achieving this requires comprehensive evaluations of the reliability of information produced by LLMs.

Worldwide, social protection programs have expanded substantially over the past decade, reaching approximately 4.7 billion people across low- and middleincome countries, although an estimated 2 billion people remain uncovered or in­ adequately covered (World Bank, 2025). Although high-income countries typically administer social assistance programs through robust institutional frameworks, low- and middle-income countries face persistent challenges related to targeting accuracy, digital inclusion, administrative capacity, and long-term sustainability (International Growth Centre, 2023; World Bank, 2025). Classi /uniFB01 ed as an uppermiddle-income country by the World Bank (2025), Türkiye offers an illustrative case in this regard. The social assistance system administered by the Ministry of Family and Social Services encompasses a wide spectrum of bene /uniFB01 ts, ranging from cash transfers to education and housing support. However, it also presents contextspeci /uniFB01 c issues concerning digital accessibility, information transparency, and the complexity of navigating program requirements. These dynamics position Tür­ kiye as a meaningful example within international debates on the digital trans­ formation of social assistance systems. The relevance of this inquiry is further underscored by the United Nations ' (2015) 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Develop­ ment. Speci /uniFB01 cally, Sustainable Development Goal 10 (Reducing Inequalities) em­ phasizes equitable access to information and services as a mechanism for reducing social, economic, and political disparities -a priority closely aligned with core values of the social work profession, including social justice, human rights, and inclusivity.

## Current Study

The present study conducts a comparative evaluation of responses generated by four widely used LLMs -ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini, and Microsoft Copi­ lot -to frequently asked questions (FAQs) regarding social assistance, as published on the of /uniFB01 cial website of the Ministry of Family and Social Services of the Republic of Türkiye (n.d.). The analysis focuses on accuracy and readability -two critical dimensions for ensuring equitable access to information in digital service environ­ ments. Although the empirical context of the study is Türkiye, the broader ques­ tion of how reliably AI-based models communicate information about public services has substantial global signi /uniFB01 cance. The widespread and cross-national nature of accuracy, clarity, and accessibility challenges in digital information provision underscores the value of evaluating LLM performance not only for understanding opportunities and limitations in Türkiye but also for informing broader public ad­ ministration contexts. This study contributes to ongoing international discussions by examining how LLMs shape access to public information and by highlighting their potential implications for digital inclusion and rights-based approaches.

## Method

## LLM Selection

I selected four contemporary and widely used LLMs based on criteria of popularity, accessibility, technical relevance, and representativeness within the current AI eco­ system: ChatGPT-3.5, ChatGPT-4o, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot. The selected mod­ els represent leading examples of market-dominant approaches. Developed by OpenAI, the ChatGPT models operate on an architecture grounded in human in­ teraction (OpenAI, 2024), and the inclusion of both the publicly accessible ChatGPT­ 3.5 and the subscription-based ChatGPT-4o enables the evaluation of potential effects of access-related cost differences on information quality. Distinguished by its multimodal structure (i.e., the ability to process multiple data modalities), Gemini represents the capacity of next-generation models developed by Google to process different types of data, such as text and images (Google DeepMind, 2024), whereas Microsoft Copilot, which is integrated with the Bing search infrastructure, is an example of generative AI ' s role in providing corporate and real-time information (Microsoft, 2024).

## Question Selection

I used a two-step methodology to identify the FAQs related to social assistance. In the /uniFB01 rst stage, I conducted a comprehensive review of 305 FAQs published on the of /uniFB01 cial website of the Ministry of Family and Social Services of Türkiye (https://www.aile.gov .tr/sss), speci /uniFB01 cally those associated with the Directorate General of Social Assistance. These questions are categorized under different social assistance programs, includ­ ing Disability and Elderly Pensions under Law No. 2022 (97 questions), Maternity

Bene /uniFB01 t (25 questions), and Family Assistance (23 questions). To maintain methodo­ logical consistency and avoid redundancy during analysis, consecutive or repetitive questions were excluded from the study.

The following criteria were used to determine the questions included in the study:

- questions that directly pertain to social assistance programs and are listed as FAQs by the Ministry of Family and Social Services;
- fundamental questions that require discrete information and can be an­ swered with a single, clear response; and
- questions that ensure broad representation across different types of social assistance.

In the second stage, I prioritized questions that could be effectively answered by LLMs without relying on advanced prompting techniques (e.g., few-shot prompt­ ing, in which the model generates outputs from a small number of examples, such as ' Give answer Y to question X ' ; or chain-of-thought prompting, which enables the model to articulate step-by-step reasoning before producing a /uniFB01 nal answer, such as ' Think step by step while answering ' ). The selected questions were in­ tended to elicit straightforward, comprehensible responses, taking into account the general cognitive and linguistic competencies of users who search for social as­ sistance information online. This approach allowed me to create a dataset that more closely mirrors real-world user experiences in assessing the accuracy and readability of LLM-generated responses. Ultimately, 20 questions that met the spec­ i /uniFB01 ed criteria were included in the /uniFB01 nal scope of the study. The 20 questions se­ lected from the initial pool of 305 items ensured adequate representativeness by providing a balanced distribution across the different social assistance programs de /uniFB01 ned by the ministry (e.g., disability, older adult, birth, and family assistance). In addition, because the study required a detailed, human-centered evaluation of 80 individual responses generated by the four LLMs -particularly in terms of ac­ curacy and readability -this sample size ( N 5 20) was most appropriate given the resource constraints and the intensive nature of the analysis. ## Readability Assessment of LLM Responses

I used the Ate ş man readability formula to quantitatively assess the readability of the LLM-generated responses. Developed by Ate ş man (1997), this formula is an ad­ aptation of Flesch ' s reading ease formula (1948) tailored for the Turkish language. It calculates readability based on the average word length (measured in syllables) and average sentence length (measured in words).

The Ate ş man formula is calculated as follows:

198 : 825 -(40 : 175 /C2 average word length) -(2 : 610 /C2 average sentence length)

Based on this formula, Turkish texts are categorized into different levels of read­ ability. Texts scoring 90 -100 are considered very easy to read, 70 -89 are easy, 50 -69 are moderately dif /uniFB01 cult, 30 -49 are dif /uniFB01 cult, and 1 -29 are very dif /uniFB01 cult (Ate ş man, 1997). The reason for selecting this formula -which is derived from the internation­ ally used Flesch -Kincaid Index -is that Flesch formulas cannot be directly applied to Turkish due to the language ' s agglutinative (synthetic/suf /uniFB01 xing) structure and natu­ rally long word forms; hence, the formula was speci /uniFB01 cally developed with these lin­ guistic characteristics in mind (Ate ş man, 1997). By recalculating Flesch ' s coef /uniFB01 cients for Turkish and accounting for these grammatical differences, the Ate ş man formula has become one of the most widely used and validated tools for assessing Turkish texts.

## Statistical Analyses

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Ver­ sion 25.0. The accuracy and readability scores were analyzed using means and stan­ dard deviations. To assess the normality of the data distribution, I examined results from the Shapiro -Wilk test as well as skewness and kurtosis values. A p -value of

less than .05 in the Shapiro-Wilk test was interpreted as evidence that the data did not follow a normal distribution. Homogeneity of variances across groups was tested using Levene ' s test, which indicated that the variances were homoge­ neous ( p &gt; : 05). Depending on distribution characteristics, I used Kruskal -Wallis and (analysis of variance) ANOVA tests to compare differences among groups.

I used eta squared ( g 2 ) to evaluate the magnitude of differences among groups and Cohen ' s (1988) criteria to evaluate effect sizes ( g 2 5 : 01 is small, .06 is medium, .14 is large). Pairwise group comparisons were performed using Bonferroni and Tukey post hoc tests. Additionally, I conducted a Spearman rank-order correlation analysis to examine the potential relationship between accuracy and readability scores. A p -value of less than .05 was considered statistically signi /uniFB01 cant within a 95% con /uniFB01 dence interval.

## Discussion

The rapid global expansion of LLMs and their emergence as primary tools for ac­ cessing information necessitate a critical examination of the accuracy and accessi­ bility of the information they generate, particularly in legislation-based domains such as social assistance. The /uniFB01 ndings indicate that LLMs exhibit inconsistent levels of accuracy and readability in their information production. This issue is especially critical for disadvantaged groups who rely on social service support, as access to accurate and comprehensible information is a prerequisite for bene /uniFB01 ting from so­ cial assistance programs and exercising one ' s rights effectively. Moreover, LLMs often provide information that is technically correct yet contextually incomplete or com­ plex, emphasizing the distinction between the formal accessibility (having access to information) and the semantic accessibility (being able to understand and use that information effectively for decision-making and action) of information. Digital in­ clusion must be evaluated not only in terms of technological access but also in rela­ tion to equitable participation at the levels of language, content, and context.

Like previous research in the medical and health /uniFB01 elds, this study ' s /uniFB01 ndings in­ dicate that LLMs generally exhibit high yet inconsistent levels of accuracy. Earlier

studies have shown that ChatGPT and similar models can provide high factual and linguistic accuracy in certain domains; however, this performance varies depending on the type of question and contextual requirements (Ayers et al., 2023; Buldur &amp; Sezer, 2024; Seth et al., 2023). Research conducted by Giannakopoulos et al. (2023) likewise reported that ChatGPT-4 produces more accurate responses than earlier ver­ sions in many cases, although all models occasionally generate incorrect or overly generalized information. Similarly, Zhang et al. (2024) found no statistically signi /uniFB01 ­ cant differences in accuracy across different models. In the present study, however, none of the evaluated models were able to provide satisfactorily accurate responses to frequently asked questions regarding social assistance in Türkiye.

LLMs exhibit varying capabilities and limitations in AI-based information gen­ eration. One of the key features that enables these models to provide up-to-date in­ formation is Internet connectivity, which enhances their ability to incorporate the latest developments and current practices into their responses through real-time searches. Among the models evaluated, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and ChatGPT-4o have Internet access, whereas ChatGPT-3.5 operates without it. Despite this limitation, ChatGPT-3.5 remains a widely used model due to its free availabil­ ity (Borovi ć et al., 2024). Although the inclusion of ChatGPT-3.5 was essential in this study because of its widespread adoption and continued accessibility to a large user base, the lack of Internet connectivity should be acknowledged as a limita­ tion, particularly in terms of response accuracy and appropriateness. These /uniFB01 ndings highlight the importance of considering information currency and access capabili­ ties when using LLMs in dynamic /uniFB01 elds such as public services, where content is fre­ quently updated. Therefore, the models ' capacities to access up-to-date data must be enhanced and supported by robust veri /uniFB01 cation mechanisms.

Although Microsoft Copilot emerged as the model with the highest accuracy score, it did not consistently demonstrate this performance across all questions, even when it cited the correct web page as its source in certain cases. This inconsistency highlights the differences and limitations among LLMs in terms of accessing and uti­ lizing existing databases. The /uniFB01 ndings indicate that the LLMs I evaluated have a limited capacity to provide accurate and consistent information based on speci /uniFB01 c sources. Furthermore, the models appear to lack the ability to effectively discern which sources are most reliable and to extract accurate information from them. For example, although Gemini occasionally cited the correct web page, its overall ac­ curacy score was lower, revealing inconsistencies in how these models retrieve and process information. These results underscore that LLMs still have limitations in ac­ curacy and reliability, especially when it comes to providing critical information about public services like social assistance. The observed shortcomings in accuracy increase the risk of individuals receiving incorrect guidance, which can impair their ability to make informed, rights-based decisions. This is not merely a technical issue; it also has signi /uniFB01 cant implications for social justice, as access to accurate information

can directly in /uniFB02 uence people ' s lives. In this context, further research is needed to im­ prove LLM reliability, along with the development of information veri /uniFB01 cation mech­ anisms that can be integrated with public service systems.

The literature has also reported mixed /uniFB01 ndings regarding the readability of texts generated by LLMs. Nevertheless, the /uniFB01 ndings of the present study are generally consistent with previous research identifying Gemini as a relatively more readable model (Seth et al., 2023; Tepe &amp; Emekli, 2024a, 2024b). The fact that the mean read­ ability scores of the responses generated by the four LLMs fall within the ' moderately dif /uniFB01 cult ' range (50 -69) aligns with trends reported in the literature (Cao et al., 2024; Srinivasan et al., 2024). This level corresponds approximately to a 9th -10th grade reading level -above the sixth-grade level recommended by the American Medical Association to ensure that health education materials are understandable to individ­ uals of all educational backgrounds (Weiss, 2003). Data from the Turkish Statistical Institute (2023) indicate that 53.8% of Türkiye ' s population has an educational at­ tainment of eighth grade or below. In this context, it is evident that the responses generated by LLMs offer limited accessibility for large segments of the population. This /uniFB01 nding points to a signi /uniFB01 cant source of inequality, particularly for individuals who face educational barriers and seek information about social assistance pro­ grams. Therefore, it can be argued that LLMs do not suf /uniFB01 ciently account for the so­ ciocultural context and the linguistic characteristics of target populations when reproducing information related to public services.

The additional analysis conducted in the current study demonstrated that the responses published on the of /uniFB01 cial website of the Ministry of Family and Social Ser­ vices are also classi /uniFB01 ed as ' dif /uniFB01 cult ' (30 -49) according to the Ate ş man Readability Index. This /uniFB01 nding indicates that readability challenges do not stem solely from the technical limitations of AI models; rather, they also highlight the need to reconsider the language policies of public institutions from an accessibility per­ spective. The readability problems observed in texts generated by LLMs may be in-terpreted as a digital re /uniFB02 ection of the structural linguistic complexities present in human-authored public content. Within this framework, the principles of plain language and clear communication need to be established as fundamental stan­ dards not only in traditional public texts but also in AI-based information systems. The low readability observed in informational content related to social assistance represents are not merely a technical shortcoming but an equity issue that must be addressed in terms of digital inclusion and rights-based communication.

In this study, no statistically signi /uniFB01 cant relationship was identi /uniFB01 ed between ac­ curacy and readability scores. This /uniFB01 nding is consistent with the results of a similar study conducted using Turkish texts (Ta ş k ı ran Tepe &amp; Aslantürk, 2025), but it di­ verges from the inverse relationship reported by Cao et al. (2024) in a study based on English-language data. This discrepancy may stem from both the structural characteristics of the Turkish language -such as its agglutinative nature, long word

forms, and complex in /uniFB02 ectional system -and differences in the architecture and training data of the models examined. Finally, the technical content of social assis­ tance, which is de /uniFB01 ned through legal and administrative terminology, creates an inherent tension between informational accuracy and linguistic simplicity. The pro­ vision of accurate information often requires more complex sentence structures, which in turn lowers readability scores. Therefore, the absence of a signi /uniFB01 cant corre­ lation between accuracy and readability indicates that LLMs struggle to balance linguistic clarity with information reliability, resulting in an information experi­ ence that may appear super /uniFB01 cially persuasive to users while remaining challenging in terms of comprehension. Within this framework, integrating human-centered feedback mechanisms and linguistic simpli /uniFB01 cation strategies into the LLM training processes may help reduce the imbalance between accuracy and readability in future applications.

Ultimately, this study emphasizes that the limitations in the accuracy and read­ ability of LLM-generated responses are not merely technical issues. Rather, they rep­ resent critical factors that in /uniFB02 uence core principles of social work, such as civic participation and rights-based empowerment. Therefore, the development of LLMs requires input not only from AI engineers but also from social work professionals to ensure that they serve the informational and empowerment needs of vulnerable populations. This approach is aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Devel­ opment Goal 10: Reduced Inequalities, as equitable access to information is a fun­ damental prerequisite for narrowing the digital divide. It also corresponds with International Federation of Social Workers (2018) calls regarding digital inclusion and technology ethics. By providing ethical guidance to safeguard the principles of human rights, justice, and inclusion throughout digital transformation pro­ cesses, the social work profession can contribute to ensuring that AI applications are not only technically reliable but also socially trustworthy and accessible.

## Limitations and Recommendations for Future Research

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the limited number of questions analyzed restricts the generalizability and depth of the /uniFB01 nd­ ings. Future research should consider using broader and more representative sets of questions to enable a more comprehensive evaluation of LLMs across various social assistance contexts.

Second, the primary limitation of our study is that the LLMs used are proprie­ tary, closed-source systems. Because the internal architectures and training data of these models are not transparent, replicability of the experimental conditions is re­ stricted. The reason open-source counterparts were not included in this study is that our work focused on commercially dominant models with publicly accessible Web interfaces. Future studies should conduct comparative evaluations with open-source LLMs to enhance transparency.

Third, the current study focused exclusively on the accuracy and readability of the model-generated responses. However, qualitative assessment criteria speci /uniFB01 c to the social work discipline -such as contextual appropriateness, ethical sensitivity, and impartiality -were excluded from the analysis. Given the multidimensional na­ ture of social work, the inclusion of these criteria in future studies would contribute to more discipline-speci /uniFB01 c and holistic /uniFB01 ndings.

Fourth, this research assessed only the performance of LLMs in the Turkish language and did not include comparative analyses across other languages, such as English. Evaluating the multilingual capabilities of LLMs would provide important insights into how language-based differences affect model performance. Future studies should incorporate cross-linguistic comparisons to better understand these variances.

Additionally, the study did not include data on user experiences. Considering that the effectiveness of AI-based systems is shaped not only by technical accuracy but also by user interaction, both qualitative and quantitative user-centered research is needed. Such studies would provide valuable insight into how these technologies are perceived and adopted within the context of social services.

Finally, although research on LLM performance is expanding rapidly, most of these studies have been concentrated in /uniFB01 elds such as medicine, health, education, and law. Empirical evaluations based on accuracy and readability criteria remain limited within the social sciences -particularly within social work. As a result, the /uniFB01 ndings of this study were primarily compared to literature in the medical and health domains, limiting the ability to draw direct comparisons within the social sciences. Accordingly, there is a growing need for systematic and comprehensive research on LLM performance in social policy, public services, and social assis­ tance -areas speci /uniFB01 c to the social sciences -which would help /uniFB01 ll a signi /uniFB01 cant gap in the current literature.

## Implications

This study represents one of the pioneering efforts to evaluate the accuracy and readability of LLM-generated responses to FAQs about social assistance in the Turkish context. The /uniFB01 ndings indicate that although LLMs can contribute to information ac­ cess in certain respects, signi /uniFB01 cant limitations remain, particularly when some mod­ els provide inaccurate or incomplete information and when average readability levels remain in the ' moderately dif /uniFB01 cult ' range. These shortcomings not only in­ crease the risk of individuals being misdirected in their attempts to access social rights but also create a structural barrier that undermines rights-based decisionmaking processes. Given the sensitive nature of social assistance services, the use of LLMs in this domain requires a careful, controlled, and ethically grounded approach. Similar concerns have been highlighted in studies conducted across other domains such as health, education, and related /uniFB01 elds, suggesting that the challenges associated with the use of LLMs in public services are of global relevance. In this regard, the

results underscore that issues of accuracy and readability in AI-generated content transcend national contexts, re /uniFB02 ecting broader debates on digital inclusion and equi­ table access to information at the international level.

This study initiates a critical discussion on the expanding role of digital technol­ ogies in social work while also highlighting the transformative potential of AI-based systems, particularly for vulnerable groups with limited access to information. However, realizing this potential depends not only on the establishment of global standards but also on the alignment of these models with local legislation, diverse languages, cultural contexts, and service delivery principles. This is crucial because the core val­ ues of social work -digital inclusion, social justice, respect for human dignity, and rights-based empowerment -demand not only technical accuracy but also the pre­ sentation of information in an accessible and comprehensible manner. Accordingly, the use of LLMs in sensitive policy domains such as social assistance should go beyond the mere transmission of information; they must be designed to reduce inequalities, enhance participation, and strengthen individuals ' capacity for rights-based decision-making while also being evaluated through the lenses of ser­ vice ethics and user safety. In this regard, the study not only situates the case of Türkiye within global debates but also offers insights into how LLMs can be har­ nessed more effectively and reliably within social work.

## Practical Implications for Social Work and Digital Inclusion

The /uniFB01 ndings of this study inform practical recommendations for social workers, so­ cial work educators, policymakers, and digital service designers.

For Service Delivery and Practitioners. LLM outputs should be used by social work­ ers not as primary sources of information but as supportive tools that assist preliminary assessment. Practitioners should verify the information provided by LLMs with current legislation and of /uniFB01 cial sources before making rights-based decisions, as doing so re­ duces the risk of misinformation and strengthens professional accountability. Ade­ quate contextual alignment and ethical use require an interdisciplinary framework in which technical developers, policymakers, and practitioners work collaboratively.

For Digital Inclusion and Service Design. The readability /uniFB01 ndings indicate that LLM-based systems do not yet provide consistent linguistic simplicity and accessi­ bility for all user groups. In digital service design, it is important to simplify content based on locally valid criteria, reduce cognitive load, and adopt participatory design approaches that account for the needs of users with different literacy levels. Such a design orientation is a fundamental condition for reducing digital inequalities in the social assistance domain.

For Policymakers and System Designers. For LLMs to be used reliably in social as­ sistance, it is essential to develop technical infrastructures that enable access to current legislation and support content veri /uniFB01 cation processes. Supporting the inclu­ sion of country-speci /uniFB01 c data and social policy expertise in model training would

enhance both accuracy and contextual alignment. Establishing institutional over­ sight mechanisms would bene /uniFB01 t the public by improving the reliability of informa­ tion and minimizing potential harms arising from inaccurate or outdated responses.

For Social Work Education. Social work education programs need to be strength­ ened with content that enables students to recognize errors in LLM outputs, critically evaluate accuracy and readability, and use AI tools responsibly within ethical bound­ aries. This study provides instructors with concrete examples for case analysis, model comparison, and ethical discussions, offering a directly applicable framework for integrating LLM literacy into social work education.

## Conclusion

When appropriately structured and developed through an empowerment perspec­ tive, LLMs can facilitate not only access to information but also contribute to the universal aims of social work in the digital age -namely, promoting social justice, ensuring equitable access, and strengthening individual empowerment. In this regard, the case of Türkiye highlights both the opportunities and challenges of dig­ italizing social assistance, offering insights with international relevance.
